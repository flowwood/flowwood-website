Ten ways to fill the gaps

Each collection sits in a 3-column grid. Its heading takes two cells, and the products fill the rest. Whenever 2 + pieces is not a multiple of 3, the last row ends in empty cells, and the sidebar stays empty below its three links. The mocks below use the real Dark oak collection (5 pieces, the worst case) and the real photos. Dashed red cells mark the gaps that remain.

01

Fitted heading

The heading takes whatever is left over: span = 3 − (pieces mod 3), or a full-width row when the pieces already fill whole rows. Dark oak gets a one-cell heading and two full rows. Soft stone and Pale & clear get a full-width heading above a full grid.

01b

Fitted heading at any count

Nobody has to know the count in advance. The build counts the pieces and gives the heading the leftover width. Desktop (3 columns): remainder 0 → heading full width, 1 → heading 2 cells, 2 → heading 1 cell. Tablet (2 columns): even → full width, odd → 1 cell. Mobile is one column, so it never has gaps. 10

Tinted collection bands

Each collection sits on a full-width band in its own tone: dark for Dark oak, warm stone for Soft stone, white for Pale & clear. The photos become framed objects, and leftover space reads as part of the composition, not as something missing.

Recommendation

Start with 1 + 9, then add 4

  • 1 · Fitted heading removes every orphan for any number of pieces, needs no new content and costs nothing on the critical path.
  • 9 · Working sidebar fills the largest blank area, using data that already exists.
  • 4 · Service tile as an extra wherever a gap remains, or as a deliberate cell, to bring custom size forward.
  • Take the spacing from 8 regardless; 96px between sections is generous for three short collections.
  • 7 is worth a separate discussion: the same piece appears in up to three collections, which makes the page feel thinner than the range is.
